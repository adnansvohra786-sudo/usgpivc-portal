Standard Operating Procedure & Risk Stratification Overview

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) standardises the execution of Ultrasound-Guided Peripheral Intravenous Cannulation (USGPIVC) across all acute admitting units, EDs, and inpatient wards within Hampshire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (HHFT). Transitioning from blind palpation to real-time ultrasound guidance significantly reduces mechanical complications, minimizes patient pain, and avoids unnecessary central line or midline escalation.

Upper Limb Vascular & Sono-Anatomical Architecture

Safe execution of USGPIVC requires precise differentiation between target superficial veins, adjacent companion arteries, and neural bundles. Target vessels must be selected according to strict depth, diameter, and trajectory parameters to optimize catheter longevity and eliminate mechanical complications.

Video Module 1

Ultrasound Machine Setup: Gain & Depth Control Teaching

Clinical Skills Video

Key Takeaways for Machine Optimization:

1. Depth Adjustment Rule:

Set depth so the target vessel occupies the upper-to-middle third of the display (typically 1.5 - 2.5 cm total depth). Avoid excessive depth which shrinks vessel resolution.

2. Gain Control Optimization:

Adjust overall gain until fluid-filled vessel lumens appear strictly anechoic (black) while surrounding connective tissue displays balanced echogenicity (grey).

Clinical Warning: Over-gaining causes lumen artifact noise (appearing grey/thrombosed), leading to misdiagnosis of occluded vessels.
1️⃣

Cephalic Vein (Forearm)

Courses along anterolateral forearm. Primary Preferred Target due to shallow predictable path, absence of adjacent named companion arteries, and high success rates.

2️⃣

Basilic Vein (Forearm)

Courses along posteromedial aspect of forearm. Excellent secondary target when straight, non-thrombosed segments > 3mm lumen diameter are identified.

⚠️

Upper Arm & Deep Zones

Brachial, deep radial/ulnar veins, and upper arm basilic/cephalic veins are RESTRICTED for specialist team PICC/Midline placement to preserve vessels and prevent nerve trauma.

Color Doppler Mode visualises relative blood flow direction relative to the probe face using the standard BART convention:

BLUE Away from Transducer
RED Towards Transducer
Clinical Pearl: A blue Doppler signal does not automatically prove a vessel is venous! Flow direction depends entirely on transducer angle. Always verify using physical compression testing.

Ideal Target Vessel Criteria

  • ✔ Depth: 5 mm to 10 mm (Maximum 15 mm / 1.5 cm from skin surface).
  • ✔ Lumen Diameter: Transverse internal caliber ≥ 3.0 mm (Preferably ≥ 5.0 mm).
  • ✔ Trajectory: Straight 20-30 mm path free of valves, tortuosity, or thrombus.
  • ✖ Avoid "Mickey Mouse" Sign: Central artery flanked by paired veins and nerves.

Procedural Execution, Geometry & Complication Verification

This section details the standardized clinical steps for USGPIVC execution under strict Aseptic Non-Touch Technique (ANTT), needle geometry, real-time dynamic tip tracking, and point-of-care verification algorithms for ambiguous punctures.

Anatomical Geometry Needle Insertion Geometry: The $45^\circ$ Equidistant Entry Rule ($d = h$)

Understanding why skin entry distance must equal target vessel depth when using a 45-degree angle of approach.

Right-Angle Equidistant Triangle Rule: Skin Entry Distance ($d$) = Vessel Depth ($h$)

Clinical Importance of Entry Distance ($d$):

1. Perfect Equidistant Trajectory ($d = h$):

When entering at $45^\circ$, the skin entry point MUST be positioned at a distance equal to the depth of the vessel center. For example, if vessel depth is 8 mm, skin entry is exactly 8 mm proximal to probe probe face center.

2. Hazards of Entry Too Close ($d < h$):

Forces an overly steep insertion angle ($>60^\circ$). This severely increases the risk of puncturing the posterior vessel wall, causing hematoma and catheter failure.

3. Hazards of Entry Too Far ($d > h$):

Requires an overly shallow angle ($<30^\circ$). The needle travels excessive soft tissue distance, risking missing the target entirely or needing ultra-long catheters.

Video Module 2

Dynamic Needle Tracking: The Vanishing Target Technique

Procedural Technique Video

Video Technique Highlights:

1. Tip vs Shaft Distinction:

In short-axis (out-of-plane) imaging, a bright hyperechoic dot can represent either the needle tip or shaft. You must actively move the probe face to confirm tip position.

2. "Vanishing" Sliding Motion:

Slide probe 1-2 mm proximally until the bright dot vanishes from the screen, then advance the needle 1-2 mm until it reappears in the lumen center.

Safety Check: Never advance the needle assembly unless you observe active movement of the tip inside the vessel lumen.

Step-by-Step Workflow: Vanishing Target Technique

In Short-Axis (Out-of-Plane) imaging, follow the 4-step dynamic sliding sequence to ensure safe lumen placement:

1

Skin Entry & Target Acquisition

Enter skin at distance $d = h$. Advance until bright hyperechoic needle tip depresses anterior vessel wall ("tenting").

2

Vanishing Target Sign

Slide probe 1-2 mm proximally along vessel path until hyperechoic dot disappears from ultrasound display.

3

Needle Advancement

Hold probe steady in new position. Advance needle assembly 1-2 mm until bright dot reappears in lumen center.

4

Catheter Seating

Repeat slide-and-advance sequence until ≥ 65% of catheter length resides within lumen before threading over needle.

45-Minute Practical Hands-On Simulation Workshop

Following the theoretical deck, trainees undergo a 45-minute practical workshop structured into three rotated simulation stations to consolidate machine configuration, needle tracking, and live volunteer anatomical scanning.

Station 1 (15 Mins) Machine & Prep

Machine Controls & Sterile Sheath Setup

Practice probe selection, depth (<3 cm) & gain adjustment, and deployment of sterile probe sheaths using single-use gel packets according to UKHSA rules.

Checklist Tasks:
Equipment: Ultrasound console, linear probe, sterile sheath kits, sterile gel sachets.
Station 2 (20 Mins) Phantom Blocks

Dynamic Needle Tracking on Phantoms

Execute short-axis target acquisition, align the 45-degree equidistant entry point, and master the Vanishing Target Technique on synthetic phantom blocks.

Checklist Tasks:
Equipment: Gelatin/silicone tissue phantoms, 18G/20G long cannulas, sharps bin.
Station 3 (10 Mins) Live Volunteers

Live Ergonomics & Anatomy Mapping

Practice live scanning on volunteer upper limbs. Establish straight line-of-sight positioning, hand stabilization, and execute compression / Color Doppler tests.

Checklist Tasks:
Equipment: Volunteer subjects, examination couches, ultrasound systems.
